# Experimental Results

Fire-risk prediction was evaluated using NASA FIRMS VIIRS/S-NPP hotspot observations and NASA POWER daily meteorological data from 2017–2023 over the Punjab–Haryana study region. Hotspot observations were gridded at 0.5° × 0.625° and paired with daily temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, precipitation, and historical detection features. The task was formulated as predicting whether at least one non-low-confidence VIIRS hotspot would occur in a grid cell on the next day. This target represents satellite-detected thermal anomalies and does not constitute confirmed wildfire or agricultural-fire occurrence.

A chronological split was used: 13,176 cell-days from 2017–2020 for training, 3,294 cell-days from 2021 for validation, and 6,588 cell-days from 2022–2023 for testing. The test set contained 3,264 positive and 3,324 negative cell-days. Reproduced average precision scores were 0.7409 for persistence, 0.5452 for climatology, 0.4369 for a hot/dry/windy heuristic, 0.8530 for logistic regression, and 0.8723 for gradient-boosted trees. The gradient-boosted model produced a test Brier score of 0.1285 and an alert-threshold precision and recall of 0.8368 and 0.7319, respectively. The block-bootstrap 95% AUPRC interval was 0.8013–0.9131.

Additional random forest and XGBoost 0.5-threshold runs yielded AUPRC values of 0.8842 and 0.8757, respectively. These auxiliary runs are not presented as replacements for the threshold-calibrated reference results. Overall, the ML models ranked next-day regional hotspot occurrence better than the baselines, but the experiment should not be interpreted as validated field-level fire prediction. It did not include a cropland mask, confirmed-fire labels, an independent spatial holdout, or forecast weather. Results therefore establish regional satellite-detection-risk ranking rather than operational fire-warning performance.
